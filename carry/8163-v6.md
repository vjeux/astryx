## Why

ListInput lets people build an ordered list of records, such as guests. Its grip grabbed every touch at once, so swiping over grips on a phone didn't scroll.

## What led to it

This is part of the UIKit-parity program, which makes Astryx's components behave like their iOS counterparts on touch screens. Moving Lab's reorders onto core's is part of it. ListInput had its own drag code, duplicating core `List`'s reorder. Following Cindy's direction (her 1272d6ae18 is here), it takes `reorderable`, the `createReorderable` object, keyed by `getItemKey`. Each record ends with core's handle ("Reorder guest 2"), after Remove and in Remove's cell, so the handle is the one element the reorder adds to a record. The handle comes with the owner's object, so a ListInput without `reorderable` loads no reorder code. Each move goes to `onReorder({key, to})`, and disabled or loading lists keep their handles inert. The list takes the session's style, so its insertion line marks a drop, and a record's place in reorder mode. Lab reaches core's reorder session through `@astryxdesign/core/internal/reorder`, which gains the session for rows that are not ListItems. `architecture:react-component-runtime` INV13 lists that session among its known public-API gaps (#8143's record change), as `spec:AST-107` FR7 asks. List and that session share the same two hooks, so the session's wiring lives in one place.

Before: `<ListInput isReorderable onChange={...} />`. After: `<ListInput reorderable={createReorderable({onReorder})} getItemKey={...} />`.

## Options and tradeoffs

- **Keep ListInput's own drag,** or reuse core's reorder and change the API.
- **Handle placement:** before Remove, or after it.

## Why this choice

Reusing core's reorder means one implementation, and ListInput gets the hold, the keyboard path, the hint and the announcements. The cost is the API change; Lab is private, so there's no changeset. The handle goes after Remove, where UIKit puts it. The design record drops the removed `reorderStyles.ts` path and its "Implementation gap" paragraph, which this closes, for Cindy's review.

## The record columns

With `reorderable`, the Add row reused the records' `auto` control track, which is empty there, so the Add button ran 56 px past the record fields. Each handle and its focus ring also ended 8 px past the list: the handle reaches into a `ListItem` row's inline inset, and a record has none. The control track is now fixed (Remove, the gap and the 28 px handle), and records set that inset to zero. So the fields column is 8 px narrower than on the previous head, and the handle stays inside the list.

## A record that stays put

A record that the owner's `isReorderable` keeps in place has no handle, so its control cell holds Remove alone. The cell centred it in the fixed control track, 16 px past every other record's Remove. The cell now starts its controls at the track's start, so every Remove sits in one column. A new fixture, `A11y/ListInput › Fixed record`, keeps its first record in place; it stays out of the visual matrix.

## How to check it

In Storybook `Lab/ListInput`:

1. On a phone, hold a grip and drag the record down two places. A flick scrolls, and a pinch zooms.
2. Tab to a grip and press Enter: a line marks the record's place, and a hint shows ↑, ↓ and Done. Use the arrow keys: the record moves each time and stays in reorder mode, and its grip takes its new name. Press Escape to end.
3. **Loading:** the grips do nothing, and dragging scrolls the page.
