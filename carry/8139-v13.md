## Why

People reorder playlists, to-do lists and settings on their phones. `List` can't do it, so apps build their own, often without a keyboard or screen-reader path.

## What led to it

This is part of the UIKit-parity program, which makes Astryx's components behave like their iOS counterparts on touch screens. `spec:AST-103` defines a reorderable List: `createReorderable({onReorder})` makes the behavior, `List` takes it as `reorderable`, and each `ListItem` with an `itemKey` gets a handle.

- A finger, on any device, holds a handle 150 ms to pick a row up, and so does a pen where the primary pointer is coarse. A mouse, or a pen where the primary pointer is fine, drags it directly.
- Activating the handle starts reorder mode. The insertion line marks the row's place, the arrow keys move the row and announce its place, and Escape ends the mode. A move that renames the row, as a list named by place does, keeps the mode.
- In reorder mode a hint below the handle shows Move up, Move down and Done, so taps or clicks alone can reorder (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7). Each does what its key does, with the same announcement, and focus stays on the handle.
- Each move calls `onReorder({key, to})` once.

```
<List reorderable={reorderable}>
  {items.map(i => <ListItem key={i.id} itemKey={i.id} label={i.name} />)}
</List>
```

This builds `spec:AST-103` FR1 (but `positions()` and `labels`) and FR2 to FR8. AST-103 gains "Implementation gap: not built" notes for FR1's `positions()` and FR4b's `attach`, beside its `labels` note. `component:List`'s readiness line says `reorderable` is stable and built, but for its `labels` and `positions`. `family:overlay-dismissal` lists the hint among its members. The UIKit table's measured reorder values and the drop model come in here with their first user. With an `animatedLayout` handle, a drag's commits stay the reorder's own. The CLI lists `createReorderable` with the hooks.

## Options and tradeoffs

- **During the drag:** slide rows aside, as UIKit does, or keep them still with a line marking the drop.
- **On touch:** pick the row up at once, or after a hold, so a flick still scrolls.
- **In the markup:** a wrapper around each row, or only the handle.
- **Moves without a drag:** a "Move before / Move after" menu, or a hint that shows them by the row.

## Why this choice

`design:ordered-collection-reordering` chose still rows and a line, because they need no layout work during the drag and look the same everywhere. The hold is UIKit's measured 150 ms. Only the handle is added (`spec:AST-103` FR5), so rows keep their layout. DEC-5 chose the hint, because it shows the moves where the row is, without opening a menu. It is the keyboard hint's own layer, and each button's tap area grows toward 44 px on touch through the shared touch hit area. The API is `spec:AST-103` FR1's: one behavior object, with no hook twin.

The dragged row's span is read once at the pick-up, beside the drag's other measurements, and reorder mode reads before it writes (`architecture:react-component-runtime` INV18: a per-frame path reads no layout, never interleaved with writes). A new Chromium case traces the crossings of a three-row touch drag: on the previous head each crossing forced a style pass, 4 in all; on this head none.
- **The touch hit area's new place** (13fab2df4a): main's #10216 moved `touchHitArea.stylex` from `utils/` to `internal/`, so the reorder handle and its engine import it from there.

## The stack review's fixes

- **Edge autoscroll is in the record** (d5c3f6be9b). The spec owners ruled it a consistency change under `spec:AST-103` FR3. FR3 gains their bullet verbatim, "a held drag near the scroll container's edge scrolls it toward that edge, faster the nearer it is, and stops at the end", and a verification row with its failure expectation. The engine keeps UIKit's measured autoscroll. It starts with the dragged row's edge 8 px inside the scroll edge, at 2 px per 20 ms tick (100 px/s), and rises to 10 px per tick (500 px/s) with that edge 22 px past it. The steps were measured on a `UITableView` on the iOS 26.2 simulator; the start inset is a guess inside the measured bracket of 3 to 12 pt.
- **One scroller walk** (a025edb68e). The engine had its own copy of core's scroll-ancestor walk. `nearestScroller`, which wraps that walk and falls back to the document's scroller, moves from the keyboard module to `utils/scrollingAncestor.ts`, and the reorder takes its scroller from there. The keyboard module re-exports it, so its importers keep their imports. The reorder's modules gain no keyboard module, a List without `reorderable` bundles to the same bytes, and the keyboard target's bundle does not grow.
- **The inline start in right-to-left** (aa6f013885). The preview and the insertion line took the row's physical left as their inline start. So in right-to-left, a row set in from the list's start drew both shifted by its inset. There, they now count the start from the list's right edge.
- **The preview joins no form or radio group** (c010af76a3). The preview was a plain clone of the row, so it kept each control's name. Laid beside the row, a copied checked radio took its group from the row's own: the row's radio showed unchecked for the whole drag, and a drop in place left none checked. Core's clone that takes no part in the page (names, ids and labels stripped, no form, inert and hidden) moves from `motion/transientCopy.ts` into its own module, `motion/inertClone.ts`, with `paintedBackground`. The swap's and the layer exit's copies keep building on it, and the transient-copy mechanism stays theirs (`architecture:react-component-runtime` INV12's boundary test, which gains a case naming the clone's users). The preview is now that clone, with its background from `paintedBackground`; both read before the pick-up's first write. A row holding a canvas that reads back empty drags as its bare box.
- **One layout at the pick-up** (6ff0ba0b44). The pick-up read the list's position and the row's box after its first writes, and the preview's first offset made its animator read its style back. Every read now comes first, the preview's first offset (none) is not written, and the preview holds its own `will-change` (`architecture:react-component-runtime` INV18).

## How to check it

In Storybook `Core/List`, on a phone or with touch emulation:

1. **Reorderable:** hold a grip, drag it two rows down and let go. The row lands there and the others glide.
2. Flick a grip: the list scrolls.
3. Tab to a grip, press Enter, press Down twice, then Escape. A screen reader reads each position.
4. Click a grip: the hint appears with ↑, ↓ and Done. Click ↓ twice, then Done. The row moves two places, and the moves stay.
5. **Reorder From Your UI:** choose Move in a row's menu. Reorder mode starts on that row.
6. **Reorder With Pinned Rows:** nothing drops above Favorites, but rows move past All mail.
7. On a desktop, drag a grip with a mouse or a pen: it moves at once, with no hold.
8. **Reorder Long List:** drag a grip to the bottom of the screen and hold it there. The list scrolls, faster nearer the edge, and stops once its end is in view.
