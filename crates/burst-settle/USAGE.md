`burst-settle --threshold T [--settle-ms S [--end T] | --settle-frames N [--force-frames]] [--key NAME] [--delta] [--format ffprobe|metadata|series] [--fps F] [--list] [--json] <file or ->` reads a screen recording's per-frame series and prints its motion bursts and where each settles. Inputs: ffprobe's JSON (`ffprobe -f lavfi -i "movie=REC,signalstats" -show_entries frame=pts_time:frame_tags=KEY -of json`), ffmpeg's `metadata=print` text (`-vf signalstats,metadata=print:key=KEY:file=OUT`), or a series of `value` or `time value` lines (`#` starts a comment, so a header needs one; `--fps` gives a timeless series its times). The format is detected unless `--format` names it. `--key` picks the tag (default `lavfi.signalstats.YDIF`, the mean change from the previous frame). Without `--delta` each frame's value is its motion, as YDIF or a change energy is; with `--delta` the motion is the value's change from the previous frame, for a level such as `lavfi.signalstats.YAVG`. A frame MOVES when its motion is over the threshold.

BY TIME (`--settle-ms S`, for a recording that writes a frame only on a change): a moving frame at most S ms after a burst's last moving frame joins the burst; quiet frames and spans with no frame both count as quiet. The burst SETTLES at its last moving frame once more than S ms pass with no moving frame. The last burst settles only when the recording's end comes more than S ms after its last moving frame; the end is `--end T`, else the JSON's `format.start_time` + `format.duration` (ffprobe writes those for the file itself, not for a `movie=` graph, so pass `--end $(ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration -of csv=p=0 REC)`). With no end, or no frame times, `--settle-ms` is an error. BY FRAMES (`--settle-frames N`, the default with N = 3, for a steady frame rate): a burst lasts until N quiet frames follow its last moving frame and SETTLES at the first of them; when the frame intervals are irregular (one over 1.5 times the median or under half of it) counting frames is refused, exit 2, unless `--force-frames`. A burst's PEAK is its first frame with the highest motion.

Each burst prints as `burst K: from START to END, peak P at T; settles at S…` or `NOT SETTLED: …` with the quiet span the end left, then a summary; with no burst it names the highest motion. `--list` prints every frame, `--json` one JSON object (`settle_ms`, `settle_frames`, `end`, and per burst `quiet_after_ms` or `quiet_after_frames`). Every number is read with Rust's float parser, so `6.17284e-06` is six millionths; a value that does not parse, a frame without the key, mixed time columns or a time that goes back is an error with its place, never a skipped frame. Exit 0 when every burst settles (or there is none), 1 when a burst has not settled by the end, 2 on a usage, read or parse error.

Checked (test data, `tests/fixtures`): a box that ffmpeg moves twice at a steady 30 fps gives bursts at frames 30–45 and 76–84, settling at 46 and 85, from the JSON and from the `metadata=print` text alike. One pixel changing by one luma step in a 450×360 frame gives YDIF `6.17284e-06`: no burst at 0.5, one at frame 30 at `1e-6`; the same value with its exponent dropped (`6.17284`, what a `[0-9.]+` pattern reads) makes a burst at 0.5. A fade read with `--key lavfi.signalstats.YAVG --delta` gives one burst at frames 31–39. The 12 Android rotation takes of #8095 (`rotate-8095/`, YAVG of `scale=270:-2,format=gray,tblend=all_mode=difference,signalstats`, frames only on a change) at `--threshold 0.4 --settle-ms 250` with each file's duration as `--end` give two bursts in 11 takes and three in narrow-fine-pointer-B (1.320–1.442 s, 1.702 s, 4.735–5.085 s); counting frames on them is refused. std only; `cargo test --release` runs these and the parser cases.
